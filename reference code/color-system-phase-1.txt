===============================================================================
0. WHAT CHANGED FROM v2
===============================================================================

  - CHROMATIC BUTTONS. v2 concluded white was the only viable CTA fill on dark
    grounds. That was true only because the ladder had one direction — every
    level mixes source toward ground, so on a dark ground every chromatic
    value darkens and dies. Adding a tint operation dissolves it. Recipes on
    neutral dark grounds now get a recognisably blue button, not a white one.
  - THE TINT IS ITS OWN SCALE, NOT THE LADDER WITH A DIFFERENT ENDPOINT. The
    two scales do not overlap and are not the same operation. Section 2b.
  - EYEBROW WAS BROKEN AND NEITHER REVIEW PASS CAUGHT IT UNTIL NOW. The brief
    puts eyebrow on the accent for dark recipes; brand on dark measures 1.48
    as readable text against a 4.5 bar. Eyebrow is a chromatic role and takes
    the tint, same as the fill.
  - CARD COVERAGE IS 70 SECTIONS, NOT 26. The hook is the existing cardStyle
    plumbing, not the Card primitive alone. Section 3d.
  - ACCENT now has a render-time fallback in addition to picker hiding. Hiding
    governs authoring; it does not govern data that already references the
    recipe.
  - The gate reports its covered set explicitly, and the residual is a number.

REJECTED IN v2, STILL REJECTED, NOT RELITIGATED
  - Muted at 70% on an evenly-stepped ladder. Conceded by the reviewer.

trigger. `renamedSectionColorRecipes` handles renames, not conditional
availability, so this needs its own path.

2. TWO SCALES
===============================================================================

There are two distinct operations. Conflating them is the mistake v2 made by
omission and the reason it concluded white buttons were inevitable.

2a. THE LADDER — source mixed toward the LOCAL GROUND

    Strong    100%     headline, primary text
    Body       86%     body copy
    Muted      76%     eyebrow, secondary copy
    Quiet      58%     metadata, passive UI — LARGE OR NON-ESSENTIAL ONLY
    Faint      20%     borders, dividers, tinted card fills

    No modifier => Strong.

    Bars:  Strong/Body/Muted >= 4.5   |   Quiet >= 3.0   |   Faint see 2c

    WHY MUTED IS 76%, SETTLED: at 70% the ladder fails AA at Muted (4.27) and
    drops Quiet under 3:1 (2.84 on surface) as soon as ink is authored light,
    which this plan permits. A ladder that is unsafe for a permitted authoring
    choice is a design defect, not something a warning should paper over.
    Evenness is measured in output — 8.88 / 6.37 / 4.96 / 3.23 is a stepped
    hierarchy — not in arithmetic tidiness of the input percentages.

2b. THE TINT — a chromatic swatch mixed toward the RECIPE'S TEXT SOURCE

    Chromatic fill      50% chromatic / 50% text source
    Chromatic text      30% chromatic / 70% text source

    Applied only where the ground is a NEUTRAL DARK (ink, dark, darkSurface).
    On light grounds the tint is 0% — brand at full strength already clears
    both bars (6.00 on page, 5.23 on surface). On chromatic grounds the role
    falls to the text source instead; see 4d.

    THE TWO SCALES DO NOT OVERLAP AND ARE NOT INTERCHANGEABLE:
      - different direction: ladder mixes toward the ground, tint mixes toward
        the text source
      - different range: the ladder's useful span is 100-20; the tint's is
        50-30
      - Faint at 20% source would be nearly the text source; Quiet at 58% is
        still too saturated for an eyebrow on darkSurface (measures 3.34
        against a 4.5 bar)

    Implementing the tint as "the same five rungs with a different endpoint"
    produces failing eyebrows or washed-out buttons depending which rung is
    picked. It needs its own two values.

    TWO VALUES PER ROLE, NOT PER RECIPE. Measured across every neutral dark
    ground, mixing brand toward white:

        % text src   colour     ink      dark     darkSurface
        ------------------------------------------------------
        0%           #175c82    2.03     1.48     1.11
        40%          #779bb4    5.01     3.65     2.73
        50%          #8dabc0    6.12     4.46     3.34    <- fill
        60%          #a3bccd    7.46     5.44     4.07
        70%          #baccd9    8.93     6.51     4.87    <- text
        80%          #d0dde6   10.65     7.76     5.80

    50% is the lowest value clearing 3.0 on all three (button edge, 1.4.11).
    70% is the lowest clearing 4.5 on all three (readable eyebrow). Two
    constants cover eight recipes; no recipe carries a hand-tuned percentage,
    which is the hand-maintained table this overhaul exists to retire.

    HONEST LIMIT: the reviewer asked for the tint to resolve against the local
    ground the way the ladder does. It cannot, in pure CSS — there is no
    contrast solver, so a value cannot be derived from a target ratio. The
    constants are therefore tuned to the WORST ground (darkSurface) and are
    consequently a little washed on ink, where 70% measures 8.93. The gate
    verifies all grounds, which is what makes fixed constants safe.

2c. SHARED RULES

    MIX IN OKLAB, NOT OKLCH — both scales. OKLCh interpolates hue on a polar
    path; mixing a chromatic swatch toward a near-neutral swings hue through
    unrelated territory:
        highlight -> page @70%:   oklab #d36561   oklch #c664a1  (magenta)
    Correctness, not preference.

    FAINT AND WCAG 1.4.11 — A DECISION, NOT AN OVERSIGHT. Faint draws card
    borders at 1.46-1.75 where 1.4.11 asks 3:1 for meaningful non-text
    boundaries. Accepted: the card is also distinguished by fill, padding and
    content grouping, so the border reinforces rather than carries. If a card
    ever renders fill-off AND border-on, the border becomes the only boundary
    and must step up to Quiet. Rule for phase 2.

    AGAINST TODAY'S BORDER. Today's serviceBorder is 1.054; Faint is ~1.55.
    Stated correctly: contrast ratios are not linear, and for near-identical
    colours what scales is the delta above 1 — 0.054 to 0.55, roughly a 10x
    increase in luminance difference. Borders go from effectively invisible to
    firmly drawn. Intended, but loud. Step 6 will show it.

3. THE ROLE TABLE — this is the system
===============================================================================

3a. EACH RECIPE DECLARES SEVEN INPUTS, NOTHING MORE

    --recipe-ground        the section's paint
    --recipe-card          the card surface on that ground
    --recipe-text          source the ladder runs from; also the tint endpoint
    --recipe-faint         source the faint level runs from
    --recipe-chromatic     the swatch the tint runs from (brand, or highlight)
    --recipe-cta-label     button label (flat)
    --recipe-tint-mode     `none` on light grounds, `on` on neutral dark,
                           `text-source` on chromatic grounds

--recipe-text doing double duty as the tint endpoint is what makes the tint
direction automatic: it is ink on light recipes and white on dark ones, so
"mix the chromatic toward the text source" lightens on dark grounds and
darkens on light ones without any recipe stating a direction.

3b. THE RESOLVED SCALES, DECLARED ONCE

    /* ladder — toward the ground */
    --text-strong: var(--recipe-text);
    --text-body:   color-mix(in oklab, var(--recipe-text) 86%, var(--recipe-ground));
    --text-muted:  color-mix(in oklab, var(--recipe-text) 76%, var(--recipe-ground));
    --text-quiet:  color-mix(in oklab, var(--recipe-text) 58%, var(--recipe-ground));
    --faint-line:  color-mix(in oklab, var(--recipe-faint) 20%, var(--recipe-ground));

    /* tint — toward the text source */
    --chroma-fill: color-mix(in oklab, var(--recipe-chromatic) 50%, var(--recipe-text));
    --chroma-text: color-mix(in oklab, var(--recipe-chromatic) 30%, var(--recipe-text));

Custom properties cannot take arguments, so this is one declaration per source
role rather than one parameterised resolver. Seven declarations replace ~136
hand-written rows.

    Body and Quiet had no --live-* consumer; without these rows the ladder has

    has to.

    The last two rows are deliberately unscoped — a few builder-only surfaces
    need a fixed dark and a fixed highlight regardless of context. Audit their
    consumers before locking this in (open question 4).

3d. CARD-SCOPED GROUND — coverage is 70 sections

Section 4c's card figures assume text ladders against the CARD. The general
case is phase 4, but phase 1 cannot publish card numbers without a minimal
version. One class:

    .recipe-card-context {
      --recipe-ground: var(--recipe-card);
      --recipe-text:   <light or dark source for this card>;
      /* AND a verbatim copy of the whole scales block — see below */
    }

CORRECTED DURING IMPLEMENTATION. An earlier draft of this section claimed the
scales "re-resolve automatically from the new ground," so that re-setting one
variable in a subtree would re-derive all five ladder levels and both tint
values for free. That is not how CSS custom properties work. A custom property
whose value contains var() is substituted at computed-value time ON THE
ELEMENT THAT DECLARES IT, and what inherits from there is the already-resolved
colour. A descendant changing --recipe-ground does not reach back into a scale
declared on an ancestor.

So any scope establishing a new ground must RE-DECLARE the entire scales
block, not just the ground. CSS has no mixin, so the duplication is mandatory.
In globals.css this is expressed as one selector list covering both the recipe
scope and .recipe-card-context, which keeps the two copies literally identical
rather than merely intended to be. Phase 4 generalises the same pattern and
inherits the same constraint — every new context is another copy of the block.

This is worth knowing before phase 4 scopes its work: "add a context" is not a
one-line change.

THE HOOK IS THE cardStyle PLUMBING, NOT THE Card PRIMITIVE.

    Card primitive users                    27 sections
    cardFill / cardBorder threaded          49 sections
    union — phase 1 coverage                70 sections
    registered in cardStyleComponents       46 components
    card-rendering sections overall        148
    RESIDUAL, uncovered in phase 1          78 sections

The 49 already compute a single card className (`cardFill === "none" &&
"!bg-transparent"`), so there is exactly one hookable element per section and
adding the class is mechanical.

A blind data-attribute sweep over the sections using `bg-bg-surface` was
considered and rejected: that class is not a card marker in this codebase. It
paints form inputs, checkbox indicators, `min-h-svh` page wrappers and pills
alongside real cards. Sweeping it would attach card colour contexts to text
inputs.

THE RESIDUAL 78 LADDER AGAINST THE SECTION GROUND, NOT THEIR CARD. On light
recipes the drift is small. On BRAND and HIGHLIGHT it is a different colour,
not a rounding difference — white@86% toward brand is #dee7ed, toward ink it
is #dcdde0. Step 6 should expect this, not discover it. Those sections paint a
card surface without being registered in `cardStyleComponents`, which is a
registration gap worth auditing on its own terms; phase 3 closes it.

6. CONTRAST GATE
===============================================================================

A PURE FUNCTION in a shared module, called from both the token-save route and
StyleGuideLiveSurface, so it fires while someone is experimenting.

    gateColorSystem(palette) -> { recipe, role, scale, ratio, bar, pass }[]

Per visible recipe, resolved against both ground and card:

    ladder Strong/Body/Muted    >= 4.5
    ladder Quiet                >= 3.0
    tint chroma-text            >= 4.5      (eyebrow)
    tint chroma-fill vs ground  >= 3.0      (button edge, 1.4.11)
    card vs ground              >= 1.15     (below this a card stops reading)

Warnings, not a hard block — the palette is user-authored and a miss may be
deliberate (HIGHLIGHT's 4.41 is one).

THE REPORT MUST STATE ITS COVERED SET. Card-context figures are true for the
70 hooked sections; the other 78 ladder against the section ground. A gate
that silently validates a model most of the codebase does not implement is
worse than no gate, because it manufactures confidence in exactly the numbers
someone would otherwise go and check. The report says which set it covers.

9. OPEN QUESTIONS
===============================================================================

  - Is raised at #f6fbff too close to white for a business whose page ground
    is already near-white? The lifted-card pattern needs a tinted page. The
    gate's 1.15 card floor catches it, but the failure mode is a palette that
    cannot use the PAGE recipe at all.
  - The tint constants (50/30) are tuned to darkSurface, the worst neutral
    dark ground. If a business authors a much darker darkSurface the constants
    are over-light on it. Worth a gate check that reports the tint's margin,
    not just pass/fail.
  - The 78 residual sections: audit whether they should be registered in
    cardStyleComponents rather than left to phase 3. Several may be
    registration oversights rather than deliberate omissions.
  - The unscoped --live-bg-dark / --live-accent rows need a consumer audit
    before being locked in as permanently unscoped.
