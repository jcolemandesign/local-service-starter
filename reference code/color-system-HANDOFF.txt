===============================================================================
1. WHAT THIS IS
===============================================================================

The colour system was five "recipes", each a hand-written table of ~17 CSS
custom properties. Eighteen sections had also carried per-recipe class maps
that drifted from each other. The overhaul replaces all of it with:

  - a small authored PALETTE (nine swatches)
  - two SCALES that derive everything else
  - eight RECIPES, each declaring nine inputs and nothing more
  - one ROLE TABLE mapping every --live-* token to a source and a scale

A section names a semantic token. The recipe decides what it resolves to.
Sections own no colour at all.

THE TWO SCALES ARE NOT INTERCHANGEABLE. This is the single most important
idea in the system and the one an unfamiliar reader gets wrong:

  LADDER  mixes a source toward the LOCAL GROUND.  100 / 86 / 76 / 58 / 20.
          Produces the text hierarchy and the faint border.
  TINT    mixes a chromatic swatch toward the RECIPE'S TEXT SOURCE.  50 / 30.
          Produces the CTA fill and the eyebrow on dark grounds.

The tint exists because the ladder has only one direction. On a dark ground
every laddered value moves toward the ground, which is right for text (the
source is already light) and fatal for a chromatic value - a brand-coloured
button darkens into the field and dies at 1.11-2.03 against a 3:1 bar. Mixing
toward the text source instead lightens on dark grounds and darkens on light
ones, with no recipe stating a direction, because --recipe-text is white on
dark recipes and ink on light ones.

An earlier draft concluded white was the only possible CTA on dark grounds.
That was wrong, and only because the tint did not exist yet.

ALL MIXING IS `in oklab`, NEVER `in oklch`. OKLCh interpolates hue on a polar
path, so the highlight red mixed toward a blue-grey ground travels through
magenta (#c664a1) where OKLab keeps it a desaturated red (#d36561). There is a
regression test for exactly this. It is correctness, not taste.

3. DECISIONS THAT WERE CONTESTED OR MEASURED
===============================================================================

Do not re-open these without re-reading the numbers. Each was argued, measured,
and settled.

MUTED IS 76%, NOT 70%.
A reviewer pushed for an evenly-stepped 100/85/70/55/20. Measured: at 70% the
ladder fails AA at Muted (4.27) and drops Quiet under 3:1 (2.84 on surface) as
soon as `ink` is authored light - which the system explicitly permits. A ladder
unsafe for a permitted authoring choice is a design defect, not something a
warning should paper over. Evenness is measured in OUTPUT (8.88 / 6.37 / 4.96 /
3.23 is a stepped hierarchy), not in tidy input percentages. The reviewer
conceded.

CARDS GET THREE INTENSITIES, BORDERS GET FIVE.
This is the opposite of what the phase 2 scope guessed. A card carries text, so
its colour must stay far enough from mid-tone that one of the two text
polarities clears AA. Measured across nine swatches x eight recipes:

    strong  72/72 clear, worst 6.37       muted  68/72, worst 4.05
    body    72/72 clear, worst 4.87       quiet  49/72, worst 2.35
    faint   70/72 clear, worst 4.17

Quiet puts a card halfway between ground and swatch where neither white nor ink
text works. So cards are offered solid / softened / wash. A border carries no
text, so all five were SAFE for it.

SUPERSEDED IN PART (session 2): the border ships TWO, not five. Contrast never
was its constraint - legibility of the control was. Strong, Body and Muted are
not tellable apart on a two-pixel line, so three of the five were a choice that
appeared to do nothing. Faint and Quiet ship, labelled Faint and Defined. The
measurement above still stands; it is the reason the CARD range is three.

INK AND DARK ARE INDEPENDENT SWATCHES.
They may be authored to the same value; nothing derives one from the other. Ink
is a text source that also grounds its own recipe; dark is a section ground.
Keeping them separate is what lets a navy band coexist with near-black body
copy later.

STORED TOKEN NAMES ARE NEVER RENAMED.
`serviceAccent` is the brand colour; `accent` is the third chromatic the system
calls `highlight`. The vocabularies disagree and the stored one cannot change -
saved slots, staged pages, page templates and approval records all carry those
keys, and the dev server rewrites several of them on its own schedule, so a
migration can race it. color-palette-adapter.ts is the single place the legacy
names stop mattering. Same rule for recipe ids: `default -> page` and
`muted -> surface` are ALIASES, not migrations.

KNOWN AND ACCEPTED CONTRAST MISSES.
  - highlight recipe, Muted eyebrow: 4.41 against 4.5
  - highlight recipe, card at Faint with a light swatch: 4.33 and 4.17

THE RECORDED EXPLANATION FOR THESE WAS WRONG (corrected session 2). It said
they were a saturated-red gamut-mapping quirk. They are not. The first is
simply where the ladder lands on a ground that starts at 6.37 - arithmetic,
fully predictable, and it would have sent the next reader to look at colour-
space conversion instead of at the ladder.

AND THE FRAMING WAS WRONG TOO, which mattered more. These were being treated
as compliance failures to be justified. The three rungs below Strong are an
EMPHASIS device - headline, then body, then muted - not four independent
accessibility contracts. Strong is the rung that carries running text. A muted
line at 4.41 on a saturated band is a de-emphasised line that reads perfectly
well, which is what it is for.

What actually needed fixing was the case where de-emphasis cost legibility:
the brand ground starts at 4.55, so the standard 58% rung landed at 2.59. See
section 9 - the spread now narrows on grounds that cannot afford it, and no
recipe carries a "miss" that has to be argued about.

PROVISIONAL, AWAITING JOSH'S EYE: the two tint constants (50 / 30) in
color-scales.ts and globals.css. Tuned to the worst neutral-dark ground, so
slightly washed on the darkest. Changing them is a two-line edit; nothing else
depends on their values.

4. TRAPS ALREADY HIT — DO NOT REPEAT
===============================================================================

CSS CUSTOM PROPERTY SUBSTITUTION HAPPENS AT THE DECLARING ELEMENT.
A custom property whose value contains var() is substituted at computed-value
time ON THE ELEMENT THAT DECLARES IT, and what inherits is the already-resolved
colour. A descendant re-pointing --recipe-ground does NOT re-resolve a scale
declared on an ancestor.

The phase 1 plan asserted the opposite for two drafts and called it "the
property the whole design rests on". No amount of review caught it; running the
CSS did. Consequence: any scope establishing a new ground must RE-DECLARE the
whole scales block. That is why one selector list covers every context, and why
a test asserts each scale is declared exactly once.

AN INVALID CUSTOM PROPERTY POISONS EVERY var() THAT READS IT.
It does not fall back to the value it shadows. This bit twice:
  1. Emitting `--live-cta-accent:` empty when unset would have invalidated
     every declaration reading it. Fixed by OMITTING the declaration.
  2. `.recipe-card-context` on the Card primitive resolved --live-* from
     --recipe-card-text, which only exists inside a recipe. The style guide
     renders swatches inside Cards with no recipe, so every swatch silently
     painted the project's hardcoded defaults instead of the palette being
     edited. The control looked broken; the cause was three elements up the
     tree. Fixed by scoping the class to inside a recipe frame or a band, with
     a test forbidding a bare `.recipe-card-context` selector.

Both failures are silent, total, and far from where they show. Suspect this
first when a colour "just isn't applying".

THE DEV SERVER WRITES REPO FILES.
`src/content/pagebuilder-options.json` and staged-page JSON are rewritten while
`npm run dev` runs. Re-check `git status` immediately before any migration, and
NEVER use `git add -A` blindly - it did sweep a builder save into an unrelated
CSS commit (8363102). See open item 4 below.

A SERVER-ONLY MODULE REACHED FROM A CLIENT COMPONENT FAILS THE BUILD, NOT THE
TYPECHECK. (Session 2.)
`promoted-palette.ts` imports `node:fs`. Turbopack fails with "the chunking
context (unknown) does not support external modules (request: node:fs)" -
which names neither the import that caused it nor the client module that
reached it. `tsc`, vitest and eslint were all green.

The chain was three deep and none of it is marked `"use client"` except the
top: StrategyWorkspaceSection (client) -> StagedPageCanvas ->
PageTemplatePreview -> promoted-palette. A direct-import check would have
passed. `promoted-palette.test.ts` now walks the whole local import graph from
every `"use client"` entry point and names the offending chain, skipping
`import type` because those are erased before bundling - without that skip it
false-positives on SiteExportControls.

The rule: preview components take the palette as a prop. Only server pages
call `readPromotedPalette()`; client surfaces call `usePromotedPalette()`.

A SPREAD ABOVE `key` SILENTLY KILLS THE KEY. (Session 2, found by Josh in the
running builder.)
Spreading the override attributes onto the section frame put a `{...spread}`
above the element's existing `key={section.id}`, and React warned "Each child
in a list should have a unique key" for every section on the canvas. Declared
after a spread, the JSX transform stops lifting `key` out into the element and
leaves it an ordinary prop. Moving `key` above the spread is the entire fix.

Both orderings look equally correct in review, and tsc, eslint and vitest were
all green - only the browser console showed it. Phase 4 will add attributes to
card elements the same way, so check the ordering there.

Also worth knowing while chasing this: the MCP console reader accumulates
messages across reloads, so a stale warning reads as a live one. Clear the
buffer, reload, then read - and confirm the page actually rendered the list
(the frames were there: 11 plus a band) or a clean console proves nothing.

GIT CHECKOUT TO UNDO A TEMPORARY EDIT WILL TAKE THE REAL WORK WITH IT.
(Session 2, self-inflicted, cost a re-do.) A deliberate one-line regression was
introduced to prove the guard test above actually fires, then reverted with
`git checkout <file>` - which discarded every uncommitted change in that file,
not just the probe. Revert the probe the same way it was made, or commit first.

TEXT-MANIPULATION SCRIPTS ON BIG FILES ARE FRAGILE.
PagebuilderShell.tsx is ~7,500 lines. Two script edits on it went wrong: one
flipped the whole file to CRLF (a 19-line change committed as 15,607), another
inserted a variable into the wrong recipe block because a stale selector still
matched. Prefer targeted Edit calls. Also: an Edit once wrote a literal NUL
byte into a sentinel string, which made git treat the file as binary - no diff,
no blame.

6. HOW TO VERIFY ANYTHING HERE
===============================================================================

The TypeScript resolver reproduces Chrome exactly - eight mixes spanning both
scales and both directions were checked against Chrome 151 and matched to the
byte. So `color-scales.ts` can be trusted for arithmetic without a browser.

To check a rendered recipe, the reliable method used throughout:
  1. `npm run dev`
  2. open a real page (/hvac-service has 15 sections, 11 with cards)
  3. clone a section into a div with `class="pagebuilder-paint-surface"` and
     `data-pagebuilder-color-recipe="<id>"` plus
     `data-pagebuilder-card-fill="solid"`
  4. read the --live-* values off a descendant with getComputedStyle, and
     convert through a 1x1 canvas - Chrome returns `oklab(...)`, not sRGB

Always stop the dev server afterwards; it writes repo files while running.

Commands: `npm test` (681), `npx tsc --noEmit`, `npx eslint src/`,

8. PHASE 4 PROBE — the colour half collapsed, the addressing half did not
===============================================================================

Measured in Chrome on /hvac-service (15 sections, 11 with cards), by staging a
real card inside a `pagebuilder-paint-surface` frame on the `dark` recipe and
driving inline custom properties on the card element.

THE CSS COST OF PER-CARD OVERRIDES IS ZERO. An inline `--recipe-card` on a card
element is picked up by the rules that already exist. No new context, no copied
scales block, no new selector. Flipping a dark card to the light `raised`
swatch:

    background                #2d5869 -> #e5f4ff
    --recipe-ground           #2d5869 -> #e5f4ff     (re-points to the card)
    --live-service-muted      #cad5d9 -> #f9fcff     (ladder re-mixes)

This works because the scales, the role table and the re-grounding rule are all
declared ON the card element by descendant selectors, so `var(--recipe-card)`
resolves against that element's own computed value - and an inline declaration
is one of its own. The phase 1 substitution trap does not bite here: the scope
that re-points the ground is the same element that re-declares the scales.

IT IS NOT FREE, BECAUSE POLARITY DOES NOT COME WITH IT. `--recipe-text` stayed
white through that flip, which is white text on a near-white card:

    without --recipe-card-text    ink 1.12   muted 1.09    (invisible)
    with    --recipe-card-text    ink 13.14  muted 6.90

So a per-card override is TWO inline properties, and the second cannot be
derived in CSS - the same wall phase 2 hit. `resolveCardPolarity` already
computes it and is browser-verified, so this is plumbing, not new logic.

NESTING WORKS, WHICH IS THE PART THE PHASE 2 SCOPE EXPECTED TO BE EXPENSIVE.
It reasoned that phase 4 was where "cards nest arbitrarily and each genuinely
establishes a new scope", implying a copied context per level. They do not need
one. A card inside an overridden card re-matches the same descendant selectors
and re-declares everything, grounding itself correctly at depth 2:

    nested card on #bf0d22     ink 6.37   muted 4.41
    outer card unchanged       #e5f4ff / #232834      (nothing compounded)

(That 4.41 is the highlight recipe's known accepted miss, so the number is
consistent with the rest of the system rather than new.)

Also confirmed: the explicit `.recipe-card-context` class form behaves
identically to the token-keyed form, and a nested card with NO override of its
own grounds to its PARENT card's colour rather than the section's - so the
"copy-per-context" worry in the phase 2 scope is already handled.

WHAT PHASE 4 ACTUALLY COSTS: ADDRESSING, NOT COLOUR.
Sections render cards from content arrays - `items.slice(0, 3).map(...)` keyed
by `item.title` - and nothing in the system gives an individual card a stable
styling identity. `cardStyleComponents` membership is per-section; every
override built so far lands on the section frame. So the real work is:

  - a way to name one card in a section that survives reordering and content
    edits (index is the obvious handle and the fragile one)
  - per-card polarity resolved and transported alongside the swatch
  - a builder control that can target a card, which is a canvas-interaction
    problem rather than a colour one

THE OLD ESTIMATE IS OBSOLETE. It was sized around building a colour context per
card. That part is done and costs nothing. Re-scope before planning.

9. THE TEXT HIERARCHY IS AN EMPHASIS DEVICE (session 2)
===============================================================================

READ THIS BEFORE TREATING A RUNG AS A COMPLIANCE FAILURE. The three rungs
below Strong exist to take a little weight off - headline, then body, then
muted - so the eye lands where it should. Strong is the rung that carries
running text. Reading each rung as an independent AA contract produces a
system that flattens its own hierarchy to satisfy a bar that was never the
point, and the first draft of this work did exactly that before being pulled

The one real constraint: de-emphasis may cost weight, never legibility.

THE LADDER HAD NO ROOM ON TIGHT GROUNDS. Every rung mixes the text source
toward the ground, so each step spends contrast. How much there is to spend
depends on how far apart the ground and its text start:

    ink          14.74      dark        10.75
    surface      11.76      darkSurface  7.74
    page         10.65      highlight    6.37
                            brand        4.55

At 14.74 the standard 86/76/58 spread is nothing. At 4.55 the faintest rung
landed at 2.59, which is not de-emphasis, it is a line you cannot read.

THE FIX: THE SPREAD NARROWS WHERE THE GROUND CANNOT AFFORD IT.
`resolveRungPercentages` bisects for the furthest the faintest rung can step
and still read, then places the other two proportionally inside what is left -
so the SHAPE of the hierarchy survives and only its span shrinks. Continuous,
not a switch, so nothing jumps as a palette is tuned. Brand goes from
86/76/58 to 89.5/81.9/68.4 and renders 4.55 / 3.98 / 3.60 / 3.01: three
distinguishable levels, none of them lost.

Every roomy ground is untouched and must stay that way - page, surface, ink,
dark, darkSurface and highlight all still emit nothing at all.

HOW IT IS PLUMBED. The three step-downs read their percentage from
`var(--rung-body, 86%)` and friends, which works because `color-mix()` takes
its percentage from a custom property - the same fact phase 2's override axes
rest on. The promoted block emits `--rung-*` only for recipes that need it,
recomputed from the palette on every promote, so it cannot go stale. Card
contexts RESET the rungs to the standard: a card is a different ground from
the section it sits on, and a brand band's ink card has all the room in the
world.

TWO THINGS CHROME CAUGHT THAT NOTHING ELSE WOULD HAVE.
  1. Rounding the emitted percentage to one decimal rounded it DOWN, below the
     value the bisection had just guaranteed - the brand's faintest rung
     rendered 2.99 against a 3.00 floor. Percentages are now rounded UP, since
     a higher percentage keeps more of the text source. A test asserts the
     floor on the EMITTED string rather than on the resolver's float, because
     the float was already right.
  2. That `var(--rung-body, 86%)` inside `color-mix()` resolves at all, with
     and without the override present. Worth checking rather than assuming,
     given this system's history with CSS that type-checks and does nothing.

Verified in Chrome per recipe: brand ground compressed, page and highlight on
the fallbacks, and every card back on 86%.
